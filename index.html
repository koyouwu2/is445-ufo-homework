UFO Sightings: Shapes and Timing

IS 445 Data Visualization | Python, Altair, and Vega-Lite

This project uses the public UFO sightings dataset from the IS 445 dataset list. Both visualizations were created in Python using Altair and Vega-Lite, from a CSV loaded directly from its public URL.

The Data The Analysis

Visualization 1: Most Common Reported UFO Shapes

My first visualization is a horizontal bar chart showing the ten most frequently reported UFO shapes. Shape is a nominal variable on the y-axis, and the number of reports is a quantitative variable on the x-axis. I use a sequential blue color scheme based on the number of reports: darker blue highlights higher counts. In the Python notebook, I cleaned the shape names, removed records missing a shape, grouped the data by shape, counted the reports, and selected the ten most common categories. This makes the rankings easier to compare than displaying every category.

Visualization 2: When Were UFO Sightings Reported?

My second visualization is a heatmap showing how reported UFO sightings vary across months and hours of the day. Month is a nominal category displayed in calendar order, hour is an ordinal variable from 0 to 23, and report count is a quantitative variable represented by the intensity of a sequential blue color scale. In Python, I parsed the sighting dates, normalized 24:00 to 00:00, excluded records without valid dates, extracted month and hour, and counted reports for each combination.

Interactivity: A country dropdown lets visitors switch between all countries and selected individual countries, helping compare patterns without making the heatmap crowded. Hovering over a cell shows the region, month, hour, and count. This filtering is additional interactivity beyond ordinary panning and zooming.

These charts summarize reported sightings in a historical dataset; they do not establish that the reported events were extraterrestrial. Counts are not adjusted for population or reporting differences.
